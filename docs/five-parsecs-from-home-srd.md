# Five Parsecs from Home
## Compact rules reference

Unofficial play aid for the supplied rulebook. Page numbers use the book’s printed numbering. Keep the rulebook handy for profiles, random tables and special cases.

### 1. Start a campaign · pp. 12–35, 63–67

1. Choose difficulty, a victory condition and a campaign crew size of **4, 5 or 6**. Crew size sets the deployment cap and the base enemy-number roll. You can recruit beyond the cap and deploy fewer.
2. Generate crew types, then backgrounds, motivations and classes as applicable. Record their ability changes, resources and contacts. Choose a captain, generate starting gear and ship details, and distribute equipment.
3. Track each character’s **Reactions** (initiative), **Speed** (movement in inches), **Combat Skill** (attack bonus), **Toughness** (damage threshold), **Savvy** (technical and other checks), and any **Luck**, XP, gear or injuries. Track credits, story points, rumours, quests, patrons, rivals, ship debt and hull at crew level.

### 2. Run a campaign turn · pp. 68–86

| Stage | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Travel** | Deal with an impending invasion first. If travelling, resolve passage or ship travel and arrival events. Establish the new world’s local conditions and contacts as applicable. |
| **Upkeep** | Pay the crew, manage ship debt and repairs, and advance sick-bay recovery. Credits can buy faster recovery. |
| **Crew tasks** | Assign one task to each available crew member, with up to two on the same task. Find a patron, train, trade, recruit, explore, track a rival, repair equipment or lay a decoy. |
| **Job offers and equipment** | Determine patron terms, deadlines and any hazards, conditions or benefits. Equip the crew from the stash. |
| **Rumours** | If you have no active quest, roll d6. A result at or below your rumour count starts a quest and clears those rumours. Rumours gained during an active quest help advance it. |
| **Choose a battle** | Roll d6 against the number of local rivals. A result at or below that count forces a randomly selected rival encounter. Otherwise choose an opportunity mission, an available patron job, an active quest mission, or a rival you successfully tracked. |

**Story points** can buy rerolls and other specified benefits. Check p. 66 for timing and limits. **Luck** is a character resource that can avert removal as a casualty and normally refreshes after battle. The optional **Stars of the Story** rules give the player additional ways to steer outcomes (p. 67).

### 3. Generate the encounter · pp. 87–111

1. Apply any **deployment conditions** and generate **notable sights** where applicable.
2. Establish the **objective** and its win condition. The rules cover fights, deliveries, elimination, protection, recovery, movement through the area, securing positions, patrols, searches, access and assaults. Use the relevant objective entry for placement and victory details.
3. Generate opponents by encounter type. For base numbers, use the **lower of 2d6** with a crew cap of 4, **1d6** with 5, or the **higher of 2d6** with 6. Apply profile and difficulty adjustments. Record weapons, behaviour, Panic range, specialists and any unique individual.
4. Set the terrain, battlefield edges and deployment. Mark objective locations and decide what counts as cover, difficult ground or blocked line of sight.

### 4. Fight the battle · pp. 37–48, 112–118

**Before round one.** The crew may attempt to seize the initiative. Roll 2d6 plus the highest crew Savvy and applicable modifiers. On 10+, each crew figure may move or fire before the first round. These shots hit only on a natural 6 (p. 112).

**Each round**

1. **Reaction roll.** Roll one d6 per crew figure and assign one result to each. A die at or below that figure’s Reactions puts them in the Quick phase. Others act in Slow.
2. **Quick → Enemy → Slow.** Choose the order of your figures within each player phase. Enemies act from nearest the player’s battlefield edge to farthest. Use their behaviour profiles for movement and the targeting rules on pp. 42–43 and 46.
3. **End phase.** Check enemy morale, withdrawal, timed effects and scenario end conditions. Optional battle events use their own timing (pp. 114–117).

**Solo enemy behaviour.** Cautious enemies seek cover and shoot from range. Aggressive enemies close quickly. Tactical enemies advance in cover and try to flank. Defensive enemies hold their half and reinforce neighbours. Rampagers rush into melee. Beasts approach under cover before pouncing. Guardians stay with their assigned figure. Check the profile for exceptions, especially heavy weapons (pp. 42–43).

**Actions and movement.** A figure may move up to Speed, then take a combat action. It may skip either. Extra movement is available when foregoing fire under the movement rules. A stunned figure can move **or** take a combat action and clears one stun after acting. Three simultaneous stuns knock a figure out. Free actions, difficult terrain, obstacles and vertical movement have separate rules (pp. 37–43).

**Shooting.** Check range and line of sight. An intervening figure blocks a shot. If a visible enemy is within 3″, a shot must target an enemy within 3″. Roll **d6 + Combat Skill** per shot.

| Target | Hit on |
|---|---:|
| Exposed within 6″ | **3+** |
| Exposed beyond 6″, or in cover within 6″ | **5+** |
| In cover beyond 6″ | **6+** |

The weapon must reach the target. Standing still while unstunned allows an aimed shot, rerolling natural 1s once. Panic fire adds two shots at half base range and exhausts that weapon’s ammunition for the battle (pp. 44, 46–47).

**Brawling.** In base contact, both roll **d6 + Combat Skill**, adding **+2 for a melee weapon** or **+1 for a pistol**. The lower result takes a hit, or both take one on a tie. A natural 6 inflicts an extra hit and a natural 1 causes the roller to suffer one (p. 45).

**Damage.** For each hit, roll **d6 + weapon Damage**. A natural 6 or a total meeting Toughness makes the target a casualty, subject to any applicable save or Luck. A surviving non-casualty hit normally pushes the target back 1″ and adds a stun. A successful save deflects the hit. Piercing bypasses armour saves but still allows screen saves (p. 46).

**Morale and victory.** At round end, roll one morale die per enemy lost to combat that round. Each die in the enemy’s Panic range makes an eligible figure bail. Fearless figures are exempt. The crew can withdraw voluntarily. Record **objective won** and **Held the Field** separately, using the scenario and end-of-battle rules (pp. 114–115).

### 5. Debrief and carry consequences forward · pp. 119–135

| Order | Resolve |
|---:|---|
| 1–2 | **Rivals and patrons.** A fight can create a rival, settle an existing feud or earn a recurring patron contact. |
| 3 | **Quest progress.** Roll d6, add rumours accumulated on the quest and subtract 2 if you failed to win. **3 or less** is a dead end, **4–6** adds a rumour, and **7+** unlocks the finale. Progress can require travel. |
| 4–7 | **Pay → battlefield finds → invasion check → loot.** Holding the field can earn a separate search roll even if you missed the objective. Invasion battles have special exceptions. |
| 8 | **Injuries.** Roll for casualties, using the separate table for Bots and Soulless. Being removed by three stuns alone does not require an injury roll. Record recovery turns, lost gear and any permanent consequences. |
| 9–11 | **XP → advanced training → purchases.** Base battle XP is 1 for a casualty, 2 for surviving without a win, or 3 for surviving a win. Apply bonuses and exceptions. Spend accumulated XP on ability increases within the stated caps. |
| 12–14 | **Campaign event → character event → galactic war progress.** Apply the results and update the campaign records before the next turn. |

### Optional ways to play

- **Story Track.** A clock advances during ordinary campaign turns and triggers authored events that modify or replace a turn. The standard campaign loop continues between those events (pp. 153–160).
- **Grid, co-op or GM-led play.** Use the grid conversion for movement and sight, the co-op appendix for sharing crew control, or the GM tools for bespoke missions, hazards, factions and civilians (pp. 147–148, 161–173).
